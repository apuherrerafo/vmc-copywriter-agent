# Headline development

## Job

Develop the opening before drafting the body. A commercial headline earns attention by making a decision, cost or gain personal; it is not a slogan generator.

## Method

1. Write the reader's current belief, the decision in play, the consequence of maintaining it, the category of action/product the reader must recognize and the changed belief the feature can credibly support. The opening package (H1 plus immediate resolving line) must make clear what the piece is about before it narrows to a specific pain. This can happen directly or because the selected pain/scene makes the product opportunity naturally inferable; it does not require a functional H1.
2. Generate twelve different **territories**. A territory changes the decision, scene, consequence or reframe; word substitutions do not count. For each surviving territory, name the intended reader reaction: `alerta`, `deseo`, `alivio`, `anticipación` or `reconocimiento incómodo`.
3. For the strongest territories, write an H1 plus the immediate resolving line. The second line must continue the emotional/decisional thought, make its consequence personal and resolve it through a verified mechanism; it cannot drop straight into a feature inventory. Discard a candidate if the resolution needs an unverified claim.
4. When the editor supplied approved examples, record the exact reference architecture, decision, consequence and VMC mechanism in the run record. Pass `reference-fidelity-grader` before `headline-grader`.
5. Draft the body only after the opening passes the applicable graders.

## Conversational runway

When an opening uses a direct economic question, it must first create a short, natural entry into the reader's situation —a recognition, observation or scene— before naming the economic tension. The purpose is to make the question feel discovered rather than fired at the reader. Do not turn this into filler, a greeting or a copied editorial phrase: the runway must add a concrete human frame and lead naturally into the decision.

The runway is never the hero's idea. The largest H1 must still carry the commercial peak: a memorable payoff, alert or reframe that makes the reader want the next line. If an introductory sentence consumes the H1 and leaves the economic/decision tension as a bland follow-up, reject it. In a layout with an eyebrow, the runway may live there; otherwise it must be compressed into the H1 without displacing its selling force.

For Vendo Mis Cosas, a blunt formulation such as `¿Cuánto dinero hay en todo lo que ya no usas?` fails when it starts at the payoff without first making the reader recognize the objects, their inaction or their place in daily life. The next candidate must build an original conversational lead-in, then land the FOMO of value immobile and resolve it with publication, expectation of sale, proposals and negotiation.

## Hard failures

- announcing the feature, category or steps instead of a reader consequence;
- relying on words such as `valor`, `oportunidad`, `dinero`, `venta` or `espacio` without a specific decision, scene or consequence;
- borrowing a claim from an approved reference;
- repeating an editor-rejected pattern with synonyms;
- treating a question mark or exclamation as intensity.
- accepting a title because it is empathetic or conceptually true when it cannot name an immediate felt reaction; formulations such as `puede ayudarte`, `puede aportar` or `segunda utilidad` are support language, never the commercial peak.
- using a particular pain as the only way to decode a broad feature or campaign when that pain does not naturally reveal the product. A headline may be general; it can make the product/action clear directly or through a scene that makes the opportunity inferable. Pain is a route, not the definition of the feature.

## Responsible persuasion

Contrast, future pacing, concrete imagery and loss aversion are useful only when they illuminate a true decision. They never justify false scarcity, buyer demand, certainty or pressure.
